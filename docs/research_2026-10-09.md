# Research notes, 2026-10-09: CPU speed and mixtures of experts for SAPC2 Track 2

Two web/literature searches (no patient data involved). Claims are as reported by the sources; items marked
*unverified* were not checked against a primary source.

## What is known about SAPC2 and SAPC1
- SAPC2 is in the NeurIPS 2026 Competition Track; prizes for systems on the latency/accuracy Pareto frontier
  ([SAP news, 2026-08-05](https://speechaccessibilityproject.beckman.illinois.edu/article/2026/08/05/speech-accessibility-project-selected-to-neural-information-processing-systems-competition)).
  No public Track 2 hardware or latency budget found; assume 2–4 x86 cores.
- SAPC1 (Interspeech 2025; Zheng et al., [arXiv:2507.22047](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.22047)): 22 teams, no
  ensembles, no streaming. Winner: Parakeet-TDT-1.1B full fine-tune + forced-alignment segmentation + checkpoint
  averaging, 8.11 % WER on Test2 (Takahashi et al., DOI 10.21437/Interspeech.2025-1484). Whisper-large-v3 FT 10.03;
  Whisper + AdaLoRA 10.51. Test speakers unseen; Parkinson's 75.9 % of test speech.
- Speaker-independent beats per-user adapters for unseen SAP speakers: 5.9 vs 8.0 % WER (Agarwal et al.,
  [arXiv:2509.15516](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.15516)).
- A public Parakeet RNN-T trained on SAPC1+2 reports 11.32 % WER on SAPC2 dev (self-reported,
  [HF](https://huggingface.co/dys-asr/parakeet-rnnt-0.6b-sapc12-syn)).

## Mixtures of experts and routing
- MAS-LoRA ([arXiv:2505.20006](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20006), accents): experts averaged uniformly beat LoRA
  and full fine-tuning in one pass; learned routers did not help. → our **soup** test.
- Hydra-LoRA per-layer token routing (Qiu et al., Forum Acusticum 2026,
  [PDF](https://dael.euracoustics.org/confs/fa2026/data/articles/000950.pdf), *unreviewed*): shared A, 4 experts,
  top-2, Whisper-large-v3 on 10 % of SAP: 10.47 → 9.29 % WER; utterance/speaker-level routing gave nothing.
- On-the-fly MoE for dysarthric speech (Hu et al., [arXiv:2505.22072](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22072)): severity-
  and gender-conditioned experts, tiny router, up to 6.4 % relative WER, RTF 0.39 vs 0.31.
- Encoder adaptation carries most of the dysarthric gain; decoder-only experts barely help
  ([arXiv:2609.02735](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02735), [arXiv:2606.31722](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31722)).
- Whisper token probabilities are overconfident; learned confidence heads beat them
  ([arXiv:2502.13446](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.13446)). Our log-prob router works but is uncalibrated.

## CPU speed
- ACFT ([futo-org/whisper-acft](https://github.com/futo-org/whisper-acft)): self-distil the model at a dynamic
  `audio_ctx` against itself at 1500; restores accuracy (VoxPopuli-de base: 22.47 → 318 → 22.31 WER). Data/GPU cost
  for large models not published. Cheaper variant: training *with* timestamps reportedly made dynamic ctx work
  ([HF](https://huggingface.co/dziaineka/whisper-small-belarusian), *unverified*).
- Quantization on x86: q4_0 3–5× faster than q5_0 on an old i5; turbo q4_0 142 s vs q8_0 179 s vs f16 1286 s
  ([whisper.cpp #3752](https://github.com/ggml-org/whisper.cpp/discussions/3752)); ARM unmeasured.
- Streaming policies: LocalAgreement ([arXiv:2307.14743](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.14743)); SimulStreaming/AlignAtt
  ([ufal/SimulStreaming](https://github.com/ufal/SimulStreaming)); CarelessWhisper
  ([arXiv:2508.12301](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.12301), CC BY-NC).
- Not for us now: Moonshine v2 (no official German), Kyutai STT (en/fr), distil-large-v3 (English), Voxtral
  Realtime 4B (too large for CPU).

## Corpora with human phone labels (third search)
SAPC2 permits external data, to be described in the submission ([FAQ](https://xiuwenz2.github.io/SAPC2-website/faqs/));
the SAP corpus itself has orthographic "what was said" transcripts and ratings but no phonetic annotation. No
pretrained phone recogniser trained on dysarthric phone labels was found (closest: Yeo et al. 2026,
[arXiv:2601.21205](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21205), universal phone recognition on dysarthric speech).

### TORGO checked on ahms (`data/external/torgo`, git-ignored; academic non-profit licence accepted by Ale)
Phone files (TIMIT set, sample-aligned) for 7 dysarthric speakers: F01 134, F03 556, F04 229, M01 381, M02 393,
M04 393, M05 461 = **2,547 utterances** (M03 none); controls MC01–04 2,683; FC01–03 none. Labels are of the actual
production, not canonical: on single-word prompts 86 % of words have more than one distinct phone sequence across
dysarthric speakers (controls 50 %), and dysarthric labels differ from control labels on 321 of 324 shared words.
So TORGO is an English dysarthric phone-level test set (and training set) with human labels.
speechocean762 in `data/external/speechocean762` (CC BY 4.0).
