# Research notes, 2026-10-10: latency levers for SAPC2 Track 2

Web research only (no models run; the benchmark on this Mac was left alone). Numbers are as the sources report them.
*unverified* means no primary source was found. *est.* means my own arithmetic from the cited numbers.

## 0. What changed since 2026-10-09: the public Track 2 leaderboard

(the official record, per the [leaderboard page](https://xiuwenz2.github.io/SAPC2-website/leaderboard/)), Test1:

| JLShen | **8.83** | 13.02 | 909 | 382 | 1291 |
| susupro | 9.25 | 14.41 | 914 | 368 | 1282 |
| smfoundation | 11.06 | 16.93 | 2324 | 2037 | 4360 |
| takagi | 13.66 | 20.11 | **400** | 154 | **555** |
| yacnx3 | 14.44 | 20.98 | 833 | 59 | 892 |
| JLShen | 14.88 | 22.96 | 515 | 173 | 687 |

| Track 1 best (offline, for reference) | 8.32 | 12.12 | – | – | – |

Implications:
- **Our system is about 7x off the frontier on latency.** Our last measurement (3.4 s TTFT-stable + 6 s TTLT = about 9.4 s)
  would be dominated unless its CER is below 8.83, the current best at any latency.
- **CER is the primary accuracy metric**, with WER secondary. Latency for the Pareto chart is the mean of TTFT-stable and
  TTLT P50; the report plots their sum ([FAQ](https://xiuwenz2.github.io/SAPC2-website/faqs/),
  [home](https://xiuwenz2.github.io/SAPC2-website/)). Our reports should lead with CER.
- **A third reject rule** ([home](https://xiuwenz2.github.io/SAPC2-website/)): the stable sentence-prefix match rate on Test1
  must be at least 1/3. Stable partials must usually be prefixes of the final, so a partial model that disagrees with the
  final model is risky.
- **Rule 2 is about determinism, not batch vs. stream.** Pass 1 (accuracy) also feeds 100 ms chunks through
  `accept_chunk` and then calls `input_finished`. The difference is that Pass 1 has no real-time pacing and runs files in
  parallel worker processes ([track2 README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/xiuwenz2/SAPC-template/main/track2_starting_kit/README.md)).
  So any final that is a pure function of the chunk sequence passes, **including a full offline re-decode inside
  `input_finished`**. The things that break rule 2 are timing-dependent logic (backpressure shedding that changes the
  final, wall-clock speculative finals) and thread-count-dependent float reductions. Pin `n_threads` to a constant;
  do not derive it from `os.cpu_count()`, since Pass 1 runs several workers in parallel.
- Hardware: "evaluated exclusively on CPUs". Core count, memory and internet access are **not published**. Docker image
  `xiuwenz2/sapc2-runtime:latest` (PyTorch 2.5.0); 15,000 s wall limit per submission (both passes). No model-size limit
  is stated. External data is allowed if described ([FAQ](https://xiuwenz2.github.io/SAPC2-website/faqs/)).
- The baseline Zipformer runs at 1.42 s total on the eval hardware. That is a useful calibration: a small streaming
  transducer gets about 1.1 s TTFT-stable there with no tuning.

## 2. Lever notes

### 1. SAP-tuned Parakeet RNN-T (offline final)
- [`dys-asr/parakeet-rnnt-0.6b-sapc12-syn`](https://huggingface.co/dys-asr/parakeet-rnnt-0.6b-sapc12-syn):
  - **Licence:** CC BY 4.0.
  - **Training:** fine-tuned from `extraordinarylab/parakeet-unified-en-0.6b` (a Transformers-format conversion with no
    card; the name matches [`nvidia/parakeet-unified-en-0.6b`](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-unified-en-0.6b)).
    Trained on SAPC1+2 train (832.6 h, 972 speakers) plus 103 h CosyVoice3 synthetic audio, 10 epochs on 16 GPUs.
  - **Accuracy:** SAPC2 dev, greedy decoding: **WER 11.32 %, CER 7.16 %**.
  - **Format:** safetensors F32 for `ParakeetForRNNT` in Transformers; no .nemo or ONNX.
  - **Latency:** no CPU numbers on the card.
- **CPU speed of the model family** ([LocalAI parakeet.cpp](https://localai.io/blog/parakeet-cpp-asr-on-cpu/), 20-core
  x86, 8 threads, batch 1):
  - RNNT-0.6B: RTFx 24.3 in NeMo/PyTorch, 34.1 in parakeet.cpp f32, 1.52x NeMo with q8_0.
  - A 6 s utterance therefore costs about 0.18–0.25 s at 8 threads (*est.*), or about 0.4–0.6 s at 2–4 threads (*est.*).
  - Microsoft (32 EPYC cores, batch 1) measured Parakeet TDT-0.6B-v3 *chunked* at RTFx 1.38 with 2.4 s delay
    ([arXiv:2604.14493](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.14493)). Naive chunked use of an offline Parakeet is slow and inaccurate.
- **Streaming with the unified base:** the NVIDIA unified model is trained jointly for offline and buffered streaming
  ([card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-unified-en-0.6b)):
  - WER: offline 5.91, 1.12 s 6.29, 0.56 s 6.52, 0.32 s 6.92, 0.16 s 8.44.
  - It is not cache-aware: left context is recomputed each chunk.
  - Whether the dys-asr offline fine-tune kept this streaming ability is *unverified*. Test it on dev with 0.56 s chunks.
- **Plan:**
  - Final = greedy offline decode of the whole (trimmed) buffer, which is deterministic.
  - Partials = re-decode the buffer every 0.3–0.5 s (about 20x cheaper than the Whisper soup) with LocalAgreement-2, or
    the unified buffered-streaming mode if it survived fine-tuning.
- **Open question:** using another participant's public SAP-trained checkpoint is allowed by licence (CC BY 4.0), but
  check with the organizers whether it counts as a permitted external model (*unverified*). We can also fine-tune the
  same base ourselves (lever 3 pipeline).

### 2. Deterministic trim plus early final
- Leaders' TTLT is 58–382 ms (table above), so a final ready at or before audio end is achievable.
- Rule-2-safe recipe:
  - Final is always computed on `audio[:cut]`, where `cut` = last frame above a fixed energy/VAD threshold plus a fixed
    pad.
  - In streaming, once `cut` has not moved for longer than the pad, start the final decode on `audio[:cut]`.
  - If later audio stays below threshold, `cut` is unchanged, so the input and the final are identical to Pass 1.
  - If speech resumes, discard and recompute.
  - Do **not** decode "audio so far" with different padding or length; with Whisper's 30 s zero padding, extra
    near-silent samples can change the output.
- Our current "speculative final after 0.3 s silence" becomes rule-2 safe only if it is formalised this way.
- Whether TTLT's "audio end oracle" is the file end or the MFA speech end is *unverified*
  ([home](https://xiuwenz2.github.io/SAPC2-website/) only says "audio end oracle time"). Either way, trailing silence in
  the file gives the decode a head start.

### 3. Cache-aware streaming transducer (Nemotron Speech Streaming EN 0.6B)
- **Model** ([card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/nemotron-speech-streaming-en-0.6b)):
  - Architecture: FastConformer-RNNT, cache-aware; 250k h US-English (ASRSet), 530k h total.
  - Licence: NVIDIA Open Model License (commercial use OK).
  - Chunk vs. Open ASR average WER: 80 ms 8.43 / 160 ms 7.67 / 560 ms 7.07 / 1120 ms 6.93.
- **Equivalence:** NeMo states that cache-aware streaming models "give the exact same outputs and accuracy as when the
  whole audio is given to the model in offline mode" ([NeMo docs](https://docs.nvidia.com/nemo-framework/user-guide/latest/nemotoolkit/asr/models.html)).
  So rule 2 holds, and stable = emitted tokens, which RNN-T greedy never revises, so the prefix rule is trivially met.
- **Multi-lookahead fine-tuning:** `att_context_size=[[70,13],[70,6],[70,1],[70,0]]` with `att_context_probs`. One model
  then gives several Pareto points at inference via `set_default_att_context_size`, which means several submissions.
- **CPU speed** ([arXiv:2604.14493](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.14493), AMD EPYC 7V12, 32 cores, batch 1):

  | variant | WER | RTFx | size |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | ONNX FP32, 0.56 s | 8.03 | 6.73 | – |
  | int8 k-quant | 8.01 | 7.25 | 1.28 GB |
  | int4 k-quant | 8.20 | 7.20 | 0.67 GB |
  | int4, 0.16 s | 8.89 | 2.87 | – |
  | PyTorch CPU streaming, 0.56 s | 7.28 | 2.46 | – |

  - Per-chunk compute at 0.56 s is about 78 ms (*est.*).
  - On 2–4 cores, expect 2–4x slower (*est.*): 0.56 s chunks still real-time; 0.16 s chunks marginal.
- **Runtimes:**
  - sherpa-onnx 1.13.4 ships a C++ runtime for this model; an app runs it as INT8 ONNX (632 MB) in real time on laptop
    CPUs ([OpenWhispr](https://openwhispr.com/blog/local-streaming-speech-to-text)).
  - A GGUF/q8_0 path exists for the multilingual 3.5 version.
- **Fine-tuning:**
  - Standard NeMo `speech_to_text_rnnt_bpe` fine-tune with the streaming config.
  - Community example: a Danish full fine-tune of Nemotron 3.5 on 10 h took CV WER from 35.8 to 16.0 % but did not
    measure streaming ([LokaalHub](https://huggingface.co/LokaalHub/nemotron-3.5-da)).
  - GPU time for 832 h × 1–2 epochs is *unverified*; guess about 1 A100-day per epoch.
- **Fallback architecture** (if we must train from a checkpoint already used by the baseline): icefall streaming
  Zipformer via sherpa-onnx. int8 at 1 thread gives RTF 0.05–0.07 at 0.32–1.28 s chunks (Korean card,
  [kangkyu](https://huggingface.co/kangkyu/icefall-asr-ko-streaming-zipformer-174m), *unverified* for English). It is
  much weaker without SAP training (baseline 34.6 CER).

### 6–9. Whisper levers (why they rank low)
- **Model size:** 874 MB at q8_0 matches large-v3-turbo's size (*inferred*). Turbo has a 32-layer encoder and a
  4-layer decoder, so cost is dominated by the encoder over the padded 30 s window.
  - Speculative decoding and distil-large-v3 (same encoder) cannot help much
    ([HF assisted generation, about 2x on decoder-bound GPU](https://huggingface.co/blog/whisper-speculative-decoding)).
  - whisper.cpp has no draft-model support (*unverified*, none found).
- **LocalAgreement:** 3.3 s latency on GPU for large-v2 ([arXiv:2307.14743](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.14743), abstract).
  The policy needs two agreeing decodes, so TTFT-stable is at least 2 partial periods plus decode time.
- **AlignAtt:** SimulStreaming is about 5x faster than Whisper-Streaming but torch-only, and its README says CPU is "too
  slow for real-time" ([repo](https://github.com/ufal/SimulStreaming)).
- **CarelessWhisper:** causal LoRA conversion with chunks under 300 ms, code released, paper CC BY-NC-SA
  ([arXiv:2508.12301](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.12301)). It would mean re-training our fine-tunes, which is out of scope
  for 14 days.
- **ACFT:** L2 self-distillation of encoder states at dynamic `audio_ctx`; released only for tiny, base and small. The
  notebooks cover medium and large, but no cost or results are published ([repo](https://github.com/futo-org/whisper-acft)).
- **Runtimes** ([faster-whisper README](https://github.com/SYSTRAN/faster-whisper), small model, 13 min audio, 8 threads,
  i7-12700K):

  | runtime | time |
  |---|---|
  | whisper.cpp fp32 | 2m05s |
  | whisper.cpp + OpenVINO | 1m45s |
  | faster-whisper fp32 | 2m37s |
  | faster-whisper int8 | 1m42s |

  So the runtime swap gains about 1.2x, and OpenVINO is Intel-only on unknown eval hardware.
- **Where Whisper still matters:** it is only on the frontier if its CER beats 8.83 (the current best streaming), at any
  latency. A Whisper submission also risks the 15,000 s wall limit with 9 s decodes per utterance (*est.*; Test1 size
  unknown).

### 10. Other English streaming models
- **Moonshine v2** ([arXiv:2602.12241](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12241)):
  - Sizes: 34M / 123M / 245M parameters.
  - Open ASR average WER 12.01 / 7.84 / 6.65.
  - Latency after VAD end on an M3: 50 / 148 / 258 ms, vs. Whisper large-v3 at 11,286 ms.
  - Encoder lookahead about 80 ms.
  - Licence unnamed ("permissive"); training code *unverified*. There is no dysarthric model, so we would need our own
    fine-tune pipeline: too costly.
- **Kyutai STT 1B en/fr:** 500 ms delay; throughput numbers are GPU-only (H100, 400 streams); no CPU benchmark found
  ([card](https://huggingface.co/kyutai/stt-1b-en_fr)). Skip.
- **Voxtral Realtime 4B:** still too large for 2–4 cores. Skip.

## 3. Recommended 14-day plan

1. **Days 1–3: lever 1 plus lever 2 ("Parakeet-SAP baseline").**
   - Wrap `dys-asr/parakeet-rnnt-0.6b-sapc12-syn` in the template `Model`.
   - Final = offline greedy on the deterministically trimmed buffer, started early per lever 2.
   - Partials = 0.4 s re-decode with LocalAgreement-2.
   - Measure CER and latency on SAPC2 dev with fixed threads (2 and 4).
   - Expected: about 7–9 CER at about 1.3–2 s total, near or on the frontier.
   - Ask the organizers whether using this checkpoint is allowed (fallback: fine-tune the unified base ourselves).
   - Submit early to get Test1 numbers (3-day turnaround).
2. **Days 3–10: lever 3 (low-latency corner).**
   - Fine-tune `nemotron-speech-streaming-en-0.6b` on SAPC train with multi-lookahead.
   - Export to sherpa-onnx int8.
   - Submit at 2–3 chunk sizes to cover the 0.5–1 s region (takagi holds 13.66 CER at 555 ms).
3. **Days 10–14: lever 4 (hybrid).**
   - Nemotron-SAP streaming partials plus Parakeet-SAP offline final.
   - Check the prefix-match rate (≥1/3) and the early-emission rate (≤5 %) on dev before submitting.
   - Keep the Whisper soup only as the German personal-model demo. For Track 2, apply only lever 5, and only if it is
     submitted at all.
